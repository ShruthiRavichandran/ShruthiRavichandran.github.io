AIrVest: A Soft Robotic Wearable for Respiratory Biofeedback

A pneumatic vest that uses gentle, rhythmic pressure to guide breathing, making respiratory motion management in MRI and radiotherapy easier on patients and more reliable for clinicians.

Alfredo De Goyeneche, Shruthi Ravichandran, Stella Yu, Miki Lustig

Publications Contact
01 · Motivation

Respiratory motion is a persistent clinical problem

Breathing is largely involuntary, and in imaging and treatment settings that creates real problems. Respiratory motion blurs MRI scans and shifts the target during radiation therapy, putting healthy tissue at risk. Current workarounds ask patients to actively control their breathing throughout long, stressful procedures.

Imaging

Motion artifacts from breathing degrade MRI quality and complicate interpretation. Navigators and breath-holds add scan time and cognitive load, which is especially difficult for anxious, elderly, or pediatric patients.

Treatment

In radiotherapy, respiratory motion shifts the tumor target, requiring larger safety margins that expose surrounding healthy tissue to radiation. Reliable motion management is a safety concern, not only a comfort one.

Limits of existing tools

Gating, breath-holds, and audio/visual biofeedback all demand sustained attention, which is unreliable in the clinic. A method that works without active patient effort would be a meaningful step forward.

Our hypothesis

Gentle, rhythmic pneumatic cues from a vest that quietly expands and contracts can guide breathing through haptic entrainment, without requiring conscious effort from the patient.

02 · Design

System overview

AIrVest combines a pneumatic wearable with real-time respiratory sensing and a feedback controller that closes the loop between measured breathing and delivered cues. The system is designed to be MRI-compatible, unobtrusive, and adaptable to each patient.

  1. 1

    Sensing

    Respiratory motion is captured through multiple modalities, including nasal airflow and MRI-native motion signals, to estimate the patient's breathing rhythm in real time without hardware the patient has to interact with.

  2. 2

    Haptic feedback

    Pneumatic chambers worn against the body deliver rhythmic pressure cues timed to a target breathing period, gently expanding and releasing to suggest a new rhythm without explicit instructions.

  3. 3

    Scanner integration

    All electronics and pneumatics sit outside the scanner bore, connected to the vest by flexible tubing. Scanner-native signals such as navigator echoes and Beat Pilot Tone can drive feedback without additional equipment in the bore.

  4. 4

    Personalized control

    Breathing responses to haptic cues vary across people. Ongoing work develops closed-loop controllers that learn each patient's response and adjust cue timing and intensity accordingly.
