Breathing with the machine, not against it

AIrVest is a soft robotic wearable that uses gentle pneumatic pressure to guide breathing — making respiratory motion management in MRI and radiotherapy easier on patients and more reliable for clinicians.

Active project
Motivation

A clinical problem hiding in plain sight

Breathing is involuntary — and in high-stakes imaging and treatment settings, that creates real problems. Respiratory motion blurs MRI scans and shifts the target during radiation therapy, putting healthy tissue at risk. The current workaround asks patients to actively control their breathing throughout long, stressful procedures.

The imaging problem

Motion artifacts from breathing degrade MRI quality and complicate scan interpretation. Navigators and breath-holds add time and cognitive load — particularly difficult for anxious, elderly, or pediatric patients.

The treatment problem

In radiotherapy, respiratory motion shifts the tumor target, requiring larger safety margins that expose healthy surrounding tissue to radiation. Reliable motion management is safety-critical, not just a quality-of-life issue.

Why existing tools fall short

Gating, breath-holds, and audio/visual biofeedback all demand sustained attention — which is unreliable in the clinic. A solution that works passively, without active patient effort, would be transformative.

Our hypothesis

Gentle, rhythmic pneumatic cues — like a vest that quietly expands and contracts — can guide breathing through haptic entrainment, without requiring any conscious effort from the patient.

Approach

Soft robotics meets clinical sensing

AIrVest combines a pneumatic wearable vest with real-time motion sensing and a feedback controller to close the loop between measured breathing and delivered cues. The system is designed from the ground up to be MRI-compatible, unobtrusive, and adaptable to each patient.

01

Sense breathing

We capture respiratory motion through multiple modalities — including nasal airflow and MRI-native motion signals — to robustly estimate a patient's natural breathing rhythm in real time, without external hardware the patient has to interact with.

02

Deliver haptic feedback

The vest delivers rhythmic pressure cues via pneumatic chambers worn against the body. The cues are timed to a target breathing period — gently expanding and releasing to suggest a new rhythm, without requiring the patient to consciously follow instructions.

03

Integrate with the scanner

All electronics and pneumatic hardware live outside the scanner bore, connected to the vest via flexible tubing. MRI-compatible sensing lets us use scanner-native signals — including navigator echoes and Beat Pilot Tone — to drive feedback without additional equipment inside the bore.

04

Adapt to the individual

Breathing responses to haptic cues vary across people. Our longer-term goal is a closed-loop controller that learns each patient's response and adjusts feedback timing and intensity accordingly — moving beyond fixed heuristics toward truly personalized respiratory guidance.

Current Directions

Where we're headed

AIrVest is an active research project. We're simultaneously developing the hardware, the control algorithms, and the clinical evaluation framework. Here's what we're actively working on.

Hardware

MRI-compatible vest design

Iterating on vest form factor, chamber geometry, and materials to maximize comfort and pressure delivery while maintaining full MRI compatibility across field strengths.

Control

Personalized closed-loop control

Developing adaptive controllers — including model predictive control approaches — that adjust to each patient's entrainment response rather than applying fixed timing heuristics.

Sensing

Scanner-native motion signals

Integrating Beat Pilot Tone and navigator echoes as primary motion sensing modalities to eliminate dependence on external sensors inside the scanner environment.

Evaluation

Imaging quality metrics

Building a systematic framework for quantifying how well AIrVest reduces motion artifacts in MRI and improves target stability in simulated radiotherapy scenarios.

Updates

Project milestones

2025
Project launched — AIrVest initiated at UC Berkeley EECS. Initial system architecture, vest prototyping, and non-MR development configuration established.
2025
MRI integration — Preliminary in-scanner deployment completed. Demonstrated compatibility with MRI environment and feasibility of scanner-native motion sensing.
2026
Pilot evaluation — Initial human subject testing conducted under IRB approval. Results informing adaptive control development and next hardware iteration.
Ongoing
Closed-loop control — Active development of personalized feedback controllers. Formal imaging characterization and expanded subject studies in progress.
